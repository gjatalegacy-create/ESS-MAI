# Claim boundary

## Established by this evidence set

- The copied v189 Quantum gate recognizes and imports its sealed NK format.
- A valid imported entry can hard-block a semantically matching later
  candidate.
- Once sealed history is required, corruption, absence, or downgrade prevents
  PRO activation in the detached end-to-end experiment.
- Native v189 tests establish the referenced Shadow validation, transaction,
  deduplication, access/frequency, and WAL behaviors within their tested scope.
- Four focal files in the POC are byte-identical to their v189 originals.
- A third-party publisher page publicly attributes the broad “Dija Negative”
  concept to Bledar Gjata and ESS-MAI on 2026-06-03.
- v189 source and focused native tests contain distinct constructive and
  rigorous-negative knowledge-write branches under one closed verdict domain.

## Not established

- Negative knowledge as a novel concept.
- Universal prevention of all repeated failures.
- A cryptographically collision-resistant seal for the current FNV format.
- A complete cross-process commit receipt from Shadow Vault to Quantum.
- Distributed consensus, hostile-machine durability, or production security.
- That every ESS-MAI runtime configuration exercises this path end-to-end.
- That the 2026-06-03 article disclosed every later Living Negative mechanism,
  supplied an immutable cryptographic timestamp, or determines legal priority.
- A symmetric positive-history re-entry experiment inside this POC.

## Publication-safe statement

> POC-006 provides executable evidence that the ESS-MAI v189 source contains a
> bounded, fail-closed verified-re-entry mechanism: required negative history
> must import successfully before PRO activation, and a known imported failure
> can hard-block a future candidate. Native tests also evidence the upstream
> typed validation, Vault transaction, deduplication, and WAL components. The
> current Boolean-only persistence signal does not yet constitute a complete
> independently verifiable cross-subsystem receipt.

This wording is intentionally narrower than the project vision.
